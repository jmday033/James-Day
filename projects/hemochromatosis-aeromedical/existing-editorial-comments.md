## 1. The discovery of hereditary hemochromatosis also permits longitudinal assessment of factors that may become operationally relevant.

PROPOSED WAIVER-REVIEW FRAMEWORK — expand the list following “The discovery of hereditary hemochromatosis also permits longitudinal assessment of factors that may become operationally relevant.”

Suggested wording: “We propose that an updated HH waiver framework explicitly document treatment phase, serial iron indices and hemoglobin, hepatic fibrosis assessment, clinically indicated cardiac and endocrine evaluation, symptoms relevant to assigned duties, and tolerance of phlebotomy. Review should also address access to treatment and laboratory monitoring during deployment, recovery after treatment, and circumstances requiring restriction and reassessment. Label this as an author proposal requiring aeromedical review, not an established or validated eligibility rule. ## 2. The case also highlights the importance of family history as an aerospace medical screening tool.

SUGGESTED INSERTION — following “The case also highlights the importance of family history as an aerospace medical screening tool.”

## 3. Case Presentation

FORMAT RECOMMENDATION — at “Case Presentation”
For Military Medicine, I recommend a commentary proposing clarification/update of HH waiver guidance, with a short illustrative case. The current manuscript reads as a case report, but Stabile et al. already described military aviators with HH and waiver decisions in 2000. Your strongest potential contribution is connecting contemporary HFE diagnosis, organ assessment, treatment phases, and UAS duties to a specific policy question.

Suggested title: “Reassessing Aeromedical Waiver Guidance for HFE-Related Hemochromatosis: Lessons From an Unmanned Aircraft Pilot.”

Suggested opening thesis: “This commentary examines whether aeromedical guidance for HFE-related hemochromatosis should more explicitly distinguish newly diagnosed iron overload requiring active treatment from stable disease managed with maintenance phlebotomy. An illustrative case in an unmanned aircraft pilot highlights the clinical and operational information needed for individualized waiver review.”

This is an editorial recommendation, not a claim that the patient currently qualifies for waiver. A case report remains reasonable if actual treatment response, follow-up, and duty/waiver disposition provide a distinctive clinical lesson. Retain appropriate patient consent and institutional review/clearance requirements when including the vignette.

### Follow-up

SOURCE NUANCE — the supplied Ong et al. study (Sci Rep. 2021;11:14654; doi:10.1038/s41598-021-94083-x) specifically discusses limitations. In 134 newly diagnosed patients, Hepascore/elastography were compared with APRI/FIB-4 classifications, and could underidentify advanced fibrosis relative to those comparators, except in the subgroup with ferritin >1000 μg/L. Do not cite it as proof that ferritin <1000 or a low elastography result universally excludes fibrosis. The ACG guideline describes ferritin <1000 as a marker of LOW RISK, not a patient-specific negative fibrosis stage. Suggested wording: “Ferritin below 1000 ng/mL suggested a lower risk of advanced fibrosis, but formal fibrosis staging was [documented result/not reported].” Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34282177/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31335359/
