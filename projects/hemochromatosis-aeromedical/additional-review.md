# Additional editorial review

Review date: 2026-09-25 (Pacific/Honolulu)

## Recommendation

Develop a commentary with a brief illustrative vignette if the authors can demonstrate a specific gap in the currently effective Navy guidance. Otherwise, a case report needs a distinctive documented treatment or aeromedical outcome. The current draft is a diagnostic case narrative with broad operational implications; it does not yet substantiate a policy change.

This pass reviewed the current manuscript and the previous comments. It adds argument and reporting improvements rather than claiming a new literature search or independent peer review. The clinical verification limits recorded in the prior comments remain in effect.

## New or expanded findings

### 1. Make the policy proposal auditable — high priority

**Location:** Aerospace Medicine Significance; currently the FAA paragraph.

A reader cannot tell precisely which Navy provision should change or whether the proposed improvement already exists in discretionary practice. Build one table with four columns: verified current provision, proposed wording/change, supporting evidence, and safeguard or unresolved question. Populate the current-provision column only after verifying the effective ARWG edition. Do not describe a categorical ban based on wording that merely discourages routine waiver recommendations.

**Suggested insertion:** “Our proposal concerns the information and treatment-phase distinctions used in waiver review. The final disposition would remain with the responsible aeromedical authority. Each proposed revision should be compared with the effective HH guidance and its existing discretionary provisions.”

### 2. Address the strongest argument for continued restrictions — high priority

**Location:** proposed policy discussion, before recommendations.

The draft emphasizes early detection and prevention but does not develop the counterargument: treatment access, monitoring reliability, transient treatment effects, and incomplete evidence on operational outcomes may justify restrictions even when disease is clinically manageable. Explicitly evaluate these concerns. Do not equate effective clinical treatment with established operational suitability.

**Suggested insertion:** “Clinical treatability alone does not establish operational suitability. Any proposal for more explicit individualized review must address the feasibility of treatment and surveillance in the assigned environment, the consequences of interrupted care, and the limited evidence linking treated HH to aviation outcomes.”

This is the authors' policy reasoning; it should be presented as such, with disease-specific evidence supplied where empirical claims are made.

### 3. Separate UAS-specific relevance from crewed-flight generalization — high priority

**Location:** UAS fatigue paragraph.

Specify only documented duties and the applicable medical standard. A ground-based operator's attention demands may be relevant, but cockpit altitude, acceleration, or aircraft egress arguments cannot automatically be transferred. Neither a general vigilance study nor reported fatigue demonstrates that this patient's performance was impaired.

**Suggested replacement:** “For this UAS operator, the relevant occupational assessment should address the actual duties, symptoms, treatment tolerance, and applicable aviation medical standard. No objective operational-performance outcome is reported in this case.”

If performance was assessed, replace the last sentence with the documented measure and result. Do not infer impairment or improvement from iron indices.

### 4. Define what would count as success after a guidance update — medium priority

**Location:** final policy paragraph.

The current conclusion invokes preservation of operational health without an observable endpoint. A proposal is stronger if it identifies outcomes that could test its assumptions, including adverse outcomes rather than retention alone.

**Suggested addition:** “Evaluation of any revised pathway should track waiver decisions, time under duty restriction, treatment interruptions, adverse treatment events, and subsequent duty limitations. Retention alone would not establish that the pathway preserves safety.”

This is a proposed evaluation plan, not a validated outcome set or a claim that these data already exist.

### 5. Avoid circular use of the case as proof of the proposed eligibility criteria — high priority

**Location:** case-to-policy transition.

The patient has a planned treatment schedule, but the manuscript does not document maintenance stability, treatment response, complete fibrosis staging, or a waiver decision. Do not derive criteria from the case and then present the same case as validating them.

**Suggested sentence:** “The case illustrates the information needed for a disposition decision; it does not validate the proposed pathway or establish that this individual met its criteria.”

### 6. Compress repeated educational content and sharpen the aim — medium priority

**Location:** Introduction, Discussion, Aerospace Medicine Significance, Conclusion.

Family history, early diagnosis, absent overt complications, and multidisciplinary care recur several times. State each once in the most useful section. Keep genotype, iron studies, MRI, and the treatment plan together in a short vignette. Use the saved space for current-policy comparison, counterarguments, and the exact proposal.

Suggested commentary allocation (planning guide, not journal requirements): introduction/aim 200–250 words; policy context 250–350; vignette 200–300; evidence and limitations 400–500; operational analysis and proposed changes 600–700; conclusion 100–150. Total body approximately 1,750–2,250 words.

### 7. Use the two supplied papers for different evidentiary purposes — medium priority

**Location:** literature discussion and references.

Stabile et al. provides historical HH/Army waiver experience, with selected cases and incomplete outcome evidence. Cresta et al. provides a contemporary model of policy argument about a different disease. They do not jointly establish that HH waiver reform is safe. Preserve this distinction in citation placement and avoid wording that makes the analogy sound like disease-specific evidence.

## Existing clinical questions still unresolved

- Was TIBC actually elevated, and what was its value/reference interval?
- Was phlebotomy only recommended or actually initiated?
- Are ferritin target 50–150 ng/mL and hold hemoglobin <10 g/dL accurate specialist orders? What was the documented rationale?
- What MRI technique and concentration basis support the 8.46 mg/g result?
- Was fibrosis formally assessed? Normal examination and iron quantification do not supply a fibrosis stage.
- What follow-up, symptom response, adverse effects, duty restrictions, and waiver disposition actually occurred?
- What exact family relationship prompted testing?

These questions require records or author clarification; editorial rewriting cannot resolve them.
