# Uber versus used car — preliminary decision memo

## Recommendation

Continue using Uber and paid rides from friends. January through July 2026 averaged approximately **4 one-way rides per month**, below the minimum whole-ride purchase thresholds.

| Purchase price | Minimum one-way rides/month to favor buying |
|---:|---:|
| $5,000 | 8 |
| $10,000 | 10 |
| $15,000 | 11 |

The underlying break-even calculations retain decimal precision; displayed decision thresholds are rounded up because a partial ride cannot make purchasing cheaper.

## Observed 2026 rides

The statements identify 20 Uber-platform rides through August 9. Six additional $50 payments to a friend were documented as transportation: March 25; April 8, 17, and 23; May 31; and August 20. Uber Eats, Uber One, and Lyft were excluded.

Across the seven complete months January–July, there were **25 rides costing $810.30**, approximately 4 rides per month and $32.41 per ride. August is incomplete but contains two rides totaling $102.70 through August 20. The year-to-date observed total is **26 rides and $913.00** through August 20 (`data/observed-rides-2026.csv`).

At the January–July pace, five years would produce about 214 rides—less than half of the 437 whole rides needed for the $5,000 car. The lower observed average fare makes continued rides more favorable than the conservative $50 assumption suggests.

## Inputs still needed

The remaining high-value inputs are vehicle-specific insurance quotes, likely repairs, registration, resale value, parking, and cash versus financing. Complete August and later statements should be added when available.

Nonfinancial factors remain separate: wait time, surge availability, toddler car-seat logistics, immediate access, reliability, and breakdown risk.

## Hypothesis check

The initial hypothesis is supported. Current use is approximately 4 one-way rides per month—about half the minimum frequency needed to justify even the $5,000 car. Purchasing would not minimize cost at the current pace.
