The Financial Opportunity Cost of Continued Navy Service

A General Internist Case

James M. Day

September 29, 2026

BUS 620 - Micro and Macro Economics

Individual Research Paper - A Global Challenge, Analyzed

Challenge and approach

The Navy needs to retain physicians without assuming that every civilian salary advantage requires an equivalent bonus. The Government Accountability Office (GAO, 2020) found that military cash compensation trailed civilian medians in 21 of 27 medical and dental specialties reviewed. Its June 2026 update still lists recommendations on replacement costs, retention data, and civilian wages as unimplemented. This continuing information gap supports evaluating compensation more carefully.

The economic question is what a physician gives up by staying. Past medical-school support is a sunk cost; future pay, pension eligibility, and working conditions affect the decision (Frank et al., 2022). This case compares an O-4 general internist leaving at 10 active-service years with remaining to 20. The physician is married, age 37, and stationed in San Diego. Marit Health's (2026) $459,057 local compensation average is a benchmark, not a matched offer. The model treats it as civilian employee cash pay and adds benefits separately. Appendix A specifies all inputs.

Both comparisons use the same O-4 longevity increases and pension base. Cash flows are discounted at 5% real and pension payments at an illustrative 3%. The latter reflects inflation protection, but alternative rates are tested. All amounts are in constant 2026 dollars; reaching retirement eligibility is assumed, not guaranteed.

Results and their meaning

The four-year gross-pay comparison produces a $636,209 present-value civilian advantage, but stops before pension eligibility. Across the full ten years, the gross civilian advantage is $1,349,196, offset by a conditional pension worth $733,228. Thus, staying costs $615,968 on the gross basis. Figure 1 shows this cost falling as retirement approaches, with break-even near 14.3 years. These calculations include longevity increases without promotion; Appendix B makes the annual arithmetic visible.

Taxes and benefits substantially change that comparison. Tax-free housing and subsistence allowances favor Navy pay, while civilian wages face income and payroll taxes. After adding employer retirement contributions and subtracting civilian insurance and health premiums, staying costs $58,419. Break-even moves to 10.9 years. This is a tax-adjusted compensation comparison, not spendable income: retirement contributions are included at face value, and later withdrawal taxes are omitted. Appendix C identifies the tax brackets and simplifications.

Figure 2 then adds retiree health coverage, bringing staying to approximately break-even. A qualifying Navy domicile without state income tax raises its value to $98,456; assumed continuation pay raises it to $114,808. A 5% civilian earnings penalty after military retirement lowers it to $29,072. These are conditional scenarios, not a confidence interval. The near-zero results should not be presented as proof that staying is financially superior.

Policy implications and supporting evidence

The gross and adjusted break-even estimates answer different questions; neither identifies when bonuses cause the largest retention response. The size of their difference motivates testing whether physicians understand total compensation. Workload, deployment, autonomy, and family needs may instead explain departures. Baseline knowledge and exit reasons should determine whether communication or changes to pay and working conditions deserve priority.

Civilian health care provides a practical precedent. NHS Employers (2022, 2025) supplies communication guides and personalized Total Reward Statements covering pay, allowances, and pensions. At United Lincolnshire Hospitals, individual retirement discussions accompanied a broader retention effort; 18 retiring staff returned in its first quarter (NHS Employers, 2020). That uncontrolled experience cannot isolate the effect of education. Randomized military and university experiments show that financial information can change retirement-plan participation, but do not establish a retention effect (Goldin et al., 2020; Duflo & Saez, 2003).

The model remains sensitive to assumptions. Increasing the pension discount rate from 3% to 5% raises the adjusted cost of staying from $58,419 to $234,384. Adding an illustrative 9.3-percentage-point pension state tax raises it to $126,609. The salary benchmark mixes employers and experience levels; promotion, deductions, retirement-account taxation, and separation risk are incompletely modeled. Individual estimates and sensitivity disclosure are essential.

Recommendation and decision rule

The Navy should adapt the NHS statement and communication plan into physician-specific counseling for officers whose obligations end within 12 months. A personalized comparison would show current cash, taxes, special pays, pension eligibility, and civilian offer assumptions. A financial counselor would use the Navy continuation-pay checklist structure (Financial Readiness Program, n.d.) to review obligations and uncertainties, then document unanswered questions. Appendix F provides the adapted materials. Existing Navy career-counseling resources offer instructional examples, although their enlisted focus requires adaptation (MyNavy HR, n.d.).

Randomize commands to enhanced counseling or usual information, balance specialty and service stage, and analyze physicians by assigned group with clustering accounted for. Measure knowledge before counseling and at six months, and actual retention at 24 months. Information can spread among colleagues, supporting command-level assignment. A prospective power calculation should determine the required commands and cohorts; initially test feasibility and comprehension if adequate recruitment is not possible.

Before enrollment, approve proposed expansion criteria: a five-percentage-point retention improvement, a 95% confidence interval excluding zero, and cost per additional physician retained for 24 months below 25% of documented specialty replacement cost. These thresholds are policy judgments, not findings from the cited programs. Include counselor and administrative costs. If knowledge improves without retention, do not expand on awareness alone; use departure reasons to reconsider pay and working conditions. Report inconclusive results without claiming success.

Appendix D Pension and present value

Define A(r,n) = [1 - (1+r)^(-n)] / r. For n remaining years, the pension value is annual pension times A(0.03,30), divided by (1.03)^n. Each remaining annual compensation gap is divided by (1.05)^t, with t starting at 1.

High-3 basic pay = ($124,819.20 + $126,118.80 + $126,118.80) / 3 = $125,685.60. The BRS annual pension is 2% times 20 years times High-3 = $50,274.24 (DoD, n.d.). The first of 30 pension payments occurs one year after retirement.

At 10 years, gross pension PV = $733,228.13. Subtracting the gross remaining pay gap of $1,349,196.04 gives -$615,967.91. Applying a flat illustrative 32% federal pension tax gives pension PV of $498,595.13. Subtracting the adjusted compensation gap of $557,014.21 gives -$58,419.08.

At each later service year, discard completed compensation years and discount the remaining gaps from that decision date. Keep the same retirement pension. Interpolate between the last negative and first positive net value. This gives 14.27 years for gross compensation and 10.90 for the adjusted comparison.

No pension probability adjustment is made. A 32% pension tax for all 30 payments is a simplification: actual rates depend on later earnings, deductions, residence, and tax law. Appendix E tests a higher combined pension-tax assumption.

The benchmark combines employer types and experience levels. The model treats it as an employee offer with separately specified benefits; an actual offer may have different hours, call, productivity risk, retirement terms, or insurance coverage. Those differences must be checked before drawing an individual conclusion.

The comparison does not estimate retention elasticity, command vacancies, replacement costs, or readiness outcomes. Financial break-even is not evidence of a bonus effect. Promotion, clinical re-entry, medical separation, future pension taxation, and personal preferences can change the result.

Appendix F Adapted counseling materials

These proposed materials adapt NHS Employers (2022, 2025), the Financial Readiness Program (n.d.) counselor checklist, and MyNavy HR (n.d.) training resources. They are designed for this pilot; they are not a validated physician-retention intervention.

F1. Personalized compensation statement. Record specialty, service years, obligation end date, retirement system, household assumptions, and the date of each pay source. Compare leaving when eligible with staying to retirement using the same time horizon. Show annual after-tax cash separately from employer contributions and pension present value. Include BAH/BAS, special pays, bonus obligations, insurance, and civilian offer terms. Show uncertainty ranges and allow the physician to correct inputs.

F2. Counseling checklist. Confirm eligibility and current benefit rules; explain contingent benefits and added service obligations; review the actual civilian offer or label any benchmark; discuss taxes and pension assumptions; ask the physician to explain the main tradeoff in their own words; record corrections, unresolved questions, and a follow-up date. A financial counselor handles the comparison, with personnel specialists verifying eligibility.

F3. Reasons for leaving. Ask the physician to rank compensation, workload, deployment, clinical practice, autonomy, location, and family needs, with space for other reasons. Ask which change could affect the decision. Record benefits knowledge separately from satisfaction so accurate understanding is not mistaken for a desire to stay.

Appendix F Delivery and evaluation plan

F4. Communication plan. The pilot lead maintains a schedule identifying audience, message, delivery channel, owner, and completion date, following NHS Employers (2022). Invite eligible physicians before their obligation ends; offer a private statement review and a short individual session; provide a written summary and a route to correct errors. Train counselors on the same material and record attendance and session duration.

F5. Knowledge measure. Before counseling and at six months, use the same five scored questions with answers checked against current rules: identify taxable versus tax-exempt pay; identify pension eligibility; explain the bonus obligation; distinguish annual cash from pension present value; and identify an assumption that could reverse the comparison. Record the score from zero to five. This proposed instrument requires comprehension testing before use.

F6. Outcome record. For each assigned physician, record command, specialty, service stage, baseline knowledge, treatment assignment, participation, six-month knowledge, and active-service status 24 months after the original obligation ends. Record separation reasons and distinguish voluntary departures from involuntary losses. Keep physicians in their assigned groups for analysis and report missing outcomes.

F7. Cost and decision record. Compare retention proportions using the prespecified analysis with command clustering. Divide incremental program cost by estimated additional physicians retained only when that estimate is positive. Report uncertainty and avoid a cost-effectiveness claim when the effect is indistinguishable from zero. The main-text expansion thresholds require approval before results are observed; source programs do not establish those numerical targets.
