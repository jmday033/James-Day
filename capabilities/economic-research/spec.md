# Physician retention model specification

## Decision and scope

Estimate the financial opportunity cost of another Navy agreement for a San Diego-based general pediatrician, general internist, or family physician using the **existing compensation package**. Compare the present value of continued Navy service with a feasible civilian path over the same period. The current model does not recommend, enter, or calculate a new retention-bonus rate. The revised research brief states the same working hypothesis; GitHub history preserves the earlier bonus hypothesis.

San Diego is the labor-market case within a global readiness problem. [Naval Medical Forces Pacific](https://www.med.navy.mil/Naval-Medical-Forces-Pacific/Region/) is headquartered there and includes commands in Hawaii, Japan, and Guam. Its [mission](https://www.med.navy.mil/Naval-Medical-Forces-Pacific/Region/Mission/) is to keep assigned medical forces staffed, trained, equipped, and ready to deploy. The pay model does not estimate how much a local physician departure changes Pacific staffing or operational readiness; those are separate outcomes to observe.

## Working hypothesis

At current pay rates, a targeted effort to make eligible primary-care physicians aware of verifiable Navy-specific nonfinancial opportunities will increase their awareness and, among physicians who value those opportunities, increase agreement acceptance or later retention relative to otherwise similar physicians who receive standard information. The estimated financial opportunity cost may moderate that response: communication alone is less likely to change a choice when the physician faces a large, credible civilian advantage. This is a prediction to test, not a measured effect.

“No additional financial compensation” means no pay increase beyond the current package. Pension, TRICARE, GI Bill eligibility, tax treatment, and existing bonuses belong in the **financial** comparison. Clinical practice, leadership, research, teaching, operational work, mission, and assignment experiences are candidate **nonfinancial** attributes. Advertising can improve awareness of real opportunities; it cannot create an opportunity that is unavailable to the physician.

## Baseline profile and inputs

The reproducible baseline is a board-certified O-4 just past 10 years of service, with dependents, at ZIP 92134. The comparison covers 2027–2030 in constant 2026 dollars. Years 1–2 use the 2026 O-4 over-10 basic-pay rate; Years 3–4 use over-12. Rank stays O-4; 2026 BAH, BAS, special pays, and civilian benchmarks are held fixed. This is an illustrative path, not a pay forecast.

Current annual retention bonuses in the modeled new FY26 four-year agreement are $35,000 for pediatrics and $48,000 each for general internal medicine and family medicine. The lab allows entry of a different **actual current agreement** rate, since contract vintage and eligibility vary. Navy annual gross cash equals twelve times monthly basic pay, BAH, and BAS, plus applicable incentive pay, board certification pay, and the current retention bonus. It includes housing and subsistence allowances before any tax adjustment.

The default civilian comparators are Marit Health published San Diego city estimates: $303,974 pediatrics, $459,057 internal medicine, and $373,038 family medicine. They are mixed-employer, self-reported city estimates, not ZIP-specific or verified civilian-only offers. The lab also offers national Doximity and Medscape sensitivity benchmarks. MGMA and AMGA require the user to enter a licensed, matched value. Never average unlike geographies, dates, compensation definitions, or practice settings without documenting why they are comparable.

For an individual estimate, obtain a written civilian offer including hours, call, location, employment type, incentives, retirement contributions, health coverage, leave, malpractice coverage and tail responsibility. Record the physician's actual Navy pay statement, agreement start and end dates, obligation, years of service, retirement system, expected further service, and promotion assumptions.

## Calculation

For each service year, calculate after-tax civilian cash plus civilian benefits that differ between the paths, less civilian costs actually paid by the physician. Subtract after-tax Navy cash plus incremental Navy benefits. Discount each year's difference to the unobligated decision date. Add only pension or education value that changes because the member stays. A positive present-value difference favors the civilian path financially; a negative one favors the Navy path financially.

The annual equivalent divides the present-value difference by the present value of one dollar paid in each year. The **nonfinancial threshold** is the positive part of the financial gap: the minimum net value the physician would have to assign to Navy-specific nonpay attributes for staying to be preferable in this simplified model. It is not an observed preference, reservation wage, or predicted retention probability.

The 12-row audit table reports each specialty and year with the Marit benchmark, current Navy cash, and current gross cash gap. The figure plots cumulative current gross cash gap by specialty. At an illustrative 5% real discount rate, with 2027 as year zero and no extra taxes or benefits priced, the four-year present-value gross cash gaps are about $139,009 for pediatrics, $668,019 for internal medicine, and $347,749 for family medicine. These are incomplete financial comparisons, not recommended bonus amounts.

This threshold describes the size of the tradeoff, not an amount a message can claim to supply. Report it by specialty and person-specific scenario alongside a separate inventory of opportunities actually accessible to the physician. Do not convert awareness or stated interest directly into a dollar value.

## Benefits and nonfinancial attributes

Compare BRS or legacy pension rights using service credit, expected future service, retirement eligibility, projected High-3, discount rate, payment period, and probability of reaching retirement. Do not count already vested rights as incremental to staying. Compare household TRICARE costs with an actual civilian health plan. Count GI Bill transfer value only when staying changes dependent eligibility or use; an earned personal entitlement is not lost merely by separating. Model civilian malpractice premium and tail only if the physician, rather than the employer, pays them and they are not already reflected in the compensation figure. Record assignment predictability, clinical opportunities, family time, mission, and geographic flexibility separately; their dollar values are not observed in salary data.

For the communication strategy, identify specific available opportunities and their eligibility conditions: protected specialty practice, leadership billets, teaching or research roles, operational experience, professional development, and assignment options that can be promised accurately. Record whether each physician already knew about, wanted, and could access the opportunity. Exclude claims that cannot be verified for the relevant billet or career stage.

The [White Coat Investor's 2012 stay-or-separate essay](https://www.whitecoatinvestor.com/should-i-stay-or-should-i-go-financial-implications-of-military-separation/) motivates a second, longer career-path sensitivity: compare earlier civilian earnings and consumption with the probability-weighted value of completing active-duty retirement. Its annuity price, tax percentages, pay schedule, and legacy pension example are historical illustrations, not current model inputs. For a physician at 10 years who stays four more years, the four-year cash horizon ends before retirement; a $0 pension entry in that narrow horizon does not mean progress toward retirement is worthless. Use current DoD rules and the individual's actual retirement system and service credit for any lifetime extension.

### Pension versus rising civilian pay in the lab

For a retirement scenario, set expected additional service long enough to reach the intended retirement date. The starting civilian salary grows at an entered **real** annual rate, and an optional marginal tax rate applies to those pay changes. Year 1 total tax remains a separate input. The lab lets the user select O-4, O-5, or O-6; 2026 DFAS basic-pay steps and San Diego BAH update with grade, while the selected grade remains fixed over the projected years. It does not assume a promotion date. The High-3 pension input must be set separately for the expected retirement grade and service path. Other pay and tax inputs stay fixed unless the user changes the scenario. Enter expected future government TSP contributions separately from the pension, and compare them with civilian employer benefits not already included in the salary benchmark.

When the horizon reaches 20 active-duty years, the lab estimates annual retired pay as projected High-3 basic pay × service years at retirement × 2.0% for BRS or 2.5% for legacy High-36, then applies the entered pension tax rate. It discounts the selected years of pension payments to the decision date, weights that value by the entered chance of receiving the pension, and shows both the present value and an annual equivalent over the service horizon. [DoD retirement rules](https://militarypay.defense.gov/Pay/Retirement/) govern the multiplier and eligibility. The year-by-year table shows **gross cash gaps**; the present-value result adds pension, taxes, TSP, health, GI Bill, and entered expenses.

This remains a conditional two-path sensitivity. It assumes Navy service throughout the selected horizon and no active-duty pension in the leave-now path; pension probability only weights the retirement stream. It does not model promotion, future special-pay changes, mortality, reserve retirement, pension accrual after already reaching 20 years, or different civilian earnings after military retirement. An individual's Statement of Service, actual retirement system, projected High-3, civilian offer, and post-retirement plan are needed before interpreting the result as personal advice.

The [2019 White Coat Investor interview with Joel Schofer](https://www.whitecoatinvestor.com/being-a-military-doctor-podcast-93/) describes MCCareer.org as an unofficial way to gather dispersed career information and job announcements. It also gives examples of leadership and training opportunities, assignment uncertainty, and specialty-specific assignment processes. The interview concerns his experience, especially emergency medicine, and supplies no causal retention estimate. For this hypothesis, record whether each primary-care physician already uses official Navy channels or MCCareer.org, what they know before the intervention, and which advertised opportunities are actually available in their specialty and career stage. Compare a targeted, verified briefing with existing information access rather than assuming physicians currently receive none. Check current official policy before describing assignment processes or benefits.

[Cronin et al. (2010)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20671458/) and [Todd et al. (2025)](https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article/191/1-2/e404/8198035) support a career-path, specialty-sensitive present-value comparison, but their accession and scholarship scenarios cannot be used as FY26 Navy retention rates. The [Army longitudinal separation study](https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article/189/Supplement_3/39/7735915) makes realized family, assignment, and duty-station conditions essential covariates and possible competing interventions. The [family-physician satisfaction comparison](https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article/189/7-8/e1414/7688228) shows why pay satisfaction should be measured separately from cash gap and observed continuation. The analysis evidence table reviews all nine studies and their transfer limits. None supplies a retention probability or monetary default for the lab.

## Economics and evaluation

The civilian path is the opportunity cost of another Navy agreement. The choice involves a bundle of pay and nonpay attributes, so a gross cash gap does not identify indifference between jobs. Current retention-bonus payments affect the marginal reward from staying; sunk training costs cannot justify future pay. A compensating wage differential may be needed for unwanted moves or loss of clinical autonomy. Labor-supply elasticity requires observed behavior after a compensation change, not a salary comparison alone.

Collect specialty-level and person-level eligibility, current bonus acceptance, agreement vintage, civilian offers where voluntarily available, assignment history, clinical practice, and separation after obligation. Measure baseline awareness, preferences, and access to the advertised opportunities. Also record realized duty-station choice, advance assignment notice, moves, deployments and family impact, mentoring, administrative support, and protected clinical work. Compare a clearly specified targeted communication group with a similar standard-information group, ideally assigned in a way that limits selection bias. Record actual access and uptake separately from exposure to a message. Predefine three outcomes: increased accurate awareness, agreement acceptance at the first unobligated decision, and retention after the agreement. Analyze whether any effect differs by estimated financial gap, specialty, rank/career stage, and actual opportunity access. Distinguish survey intentions from observed acceptance and separation. Account for the separate FY25–FY26 internal-medicine pay change. Where feasible, compare communication alone with a documented improvement in assignment or clinical opportunity; do not attribute the effect of a real change to advertising. Compare communication cost with any causal additional retention; the current model does not establish the program effect or cost effectiveness.

For the Pacific-readiness rationale, collect specialty-specific billet requirements, vacancies, time to fill, deployability, clinical capacity, and assignment destinations for the relevant San Diego commands. Report whether retained physicians fill positions that support deployable forces. These measures test the staffing and readiness links; do not infer them from salary gaps or DOD-wide staffing totals.

The hypothesis would be weakened if awareness rose but acceptance and later retention did not improve relative to a comparable group, or if the advertised opportunities were rarely accessible in practice. A small effect for physicians with the largest verified financial gaps would limit the strategy's scope. If improved assignment predictability or clinical access changes observed retention while communication alone does not, recommend the operational change. The obvious objection is that information cannot replace compensation or an unavailable opportunity. The narrower claim is that better information may matter for physicians who already place substantial value on attainable Navy opportunities, while the model quantifies the financial tradeoff they face.

## Validation and limits

Recalculate the three Year 1 current gaps ($40,008.24, $182,091.24, $96,072.24), all 12 rows, the four-year cumulative gaps ($148,793.76, $717,125.76, $373,049.76), and the 5% present values above. Changing benchmark source, specialty, years, dependents, tax amounts, physician-paid costs, GI Bill assumptions, or pension switch must recalculate results. A missing MGMA or AMGA input must display no numeric comparison. Verify that no proposed-bonus input or proposed-pay result appears in the lab.

The main threats are Marit's small mixed-employer local sample, national comparator geography, unknown actual offers, tax and benefit assumptions, promotion, agreement vintage, and selection into bonus acceptance. [GAO's incentive review](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-165) identifies gaps in replacement-cost, bonus-acceptance, and civilian-wage data. [GAO's staffing review](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-106988) gives DOD-wide context rather than a local Navy primary-care retention rate. Internal survey projections should be labeled as preliminary stated intentions, kept out of public figures unless release is approved, and checked against observed separations.
