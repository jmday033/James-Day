## Decision rule for the lab and paper

The **financial opportunity cost of staying** is the present value of feasible civilian compensation and benefits, less civilian costs, minus the present value of incremental Navy compensation and benefits over the same horizon. A positive number is a civilian financial advantage. A physician might still prefer Navy service because of personally valued nonfinancial job attributes. The lab's “nonfinancial value required” is the value that would close that modeled financial difference; it is a threshold, not an observed preference or a bonus recommendation.

For any additional retention measure, compare its **marginal expected benefit** (additional physicians who actually remain, and any measured staffing/readiness benefit) with its **marginal cost** (new fiscal spending and opportunity cost of funds). A message about an opportunity has value only if it changes awareness of something real and accessible. A new assignment or clinical option must be evaluated as an actual change in the job bundle. Neither the scans nor the lab identify a causal retention effect.
