# Compensation education and retention: source review

Research notes for the policy recommendation (a total-compensation communication pilot). AI-assisted source research; findings are summarized from the sources, not author conclusions. Verify figures against the originals before citing.

## Why education might matter: people undervalue deferred pay

| Source | Setting | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| [Warner & Pleeter (2001), *American Economic Review* 91(1)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.91.1.33) | 65,000+ military separatees, 1990s drawdown, choice of lump sum vs. annuity | Most chose the lump sum even though the annuity was worth more at any discount rate below about 17%; estimated personal discount rates ranged from 0 to over 30% and varied with education, age, and payment size. Saved taxpayers about $1.7 billion. |
| [Fitzpatrick (2015); Johnston (2019), summarized in Hoover brief](https://hoover-s3-website.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/research/docs/fitzpatrick_webreadypdf_revised.pdf) | Illinois and Houston public school teachers | Teachers valued a dollar of pension at about 20 cents (Illinois) and 60 cents at the margin (Houston). |
| [UK Armed Forces Continuous Attitude Survey 2025 (Forces Pension Society summary)](https://forcespensionsociety.org/2025/05/armed-forces-continuous-attitude-survey-2025-results/) | UK regular forces | 40% satisfied with pension benefits (officers 62%, other ranks 34%). |

**Caution for the paper:** high personal discount rates may reflect genuine time preference, not only missing information. Education can fix misinformation; it cannot change preferences.

## Measured effects of benefit information

| Source | Intervention | Knowledge effect | Behavior effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Mastrobuoni (2011), CRR brief](https://crr.bc.edu/do-social-security-statements-affect-knowledge-and-behavior/) | Annual Social Security Statement (staggered rollout) | Recipients about 20 percentage points more likely to estimate benefits; estimates more accurate | No detectable change in retirement behavior |
| [Liebman & Luttmer (2015), reviewed in Smith (2020), *Social Security Bulletin* 80(4)](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/ssb/v80n4/v80n4p1.html) | **Randomized** mailing plus 15-minute online tutorial, 2,500 workers aged 60–65 | Improved | 4.2 percentage points more likely to be working at one year; no claiming effect |
| [Smith (2020), *Social Security Bulletin* 80(4)](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/ssb/v80n4/v80n4p1.html) | Receipt of multiple Statements | Improved | Age-62 claiming −3.4 pp; age-65 claiming +4.6 pp; employment ages 62–70 +5 to 7 pp |
| [NHS Total Reward Statements (NHS Employers)](https://www.nhsemployers.org/articles/total-reward-statements-centre-staff-experience) | Annual personalized statement incl. pension value, all NHS staff including physicians | Anecdotal only | Not measured |

**Takeaway:** information reliably improves knowledge; behavior effects are small (a few percentage points) and not always present. A pilot should be powered for small effects.

## Military retention incentives: what cash buys

| Source | Program | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [IDA, *Assessing the Economic Efficiency of the 2007 U.S. Army Menu of Incentives Program*](https://www.ida.org/-/media/feature/publications/a/as/assessing-the-economic-efficiency-of-the-2007-us-army-menu-of-incentives-program/p-22820.ashx) | Captains chose a $25k–$35k bonus, graduate school, training, branch, or post for 3 more years | 68% accepted; over 90% chose cash. About $92,000 per additional year of service; 62% of cost was paid to officers who would have stayed anyway. |
| [RAND MG-866 (2009), Air Force physician and dentist multiyear special pay](https://www.rand.org/pubs/monographs/MG866.html) | Multiyear retention bonuses | Higher bonus levels raised acceptance (dose-response); most eligible physicians had declined, military-trained physicians increasingly accepted. |
| [Mundell (2010), RAND dissertation RGSD-275](https://www.rand.org/pubs/rgs_dissertations/RGSD275.html) | Military physician retention | Practice opportunity, residency type, and deployment history affect retention alongside pay. |
| [GAO-20-165](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-165) (already cited) | DoD physician incentives | Military cash trailed civilian medians in 21 of 27 specialties; recommended better data. |

**Takeaway for targeting:** untargeted bonuses pay large rents to people who would stay. Targeting the career window where the decision is close (about 8–14 years in this project's model) should lower cost per retained year.

## Adaptable pilot templates

1. **Randomized mailing plus short session (Liebman & Luttmer design).** Randomize physicians 6–18 months from obligation end to a personalized after-tax total-compensation statement and a 15-minute session versus standard information. Outcomes: knowledge at 1 month, bonus acceptance at 12 months, retention at 24 months.
2. **Staggered rollout (Social Security Statement design).** Introduce statements command by command or by quarter; compare early and late groups. Easier to implement than individual randomization; weaker causal claim.
3. **Statement content (NHS Total Reward Statement model).** Annual statement showing cash pay, tax-free allowances, the tax value of state residency, TSP match, pension present value at 20, retiree health value, and a break-even estimate.
4. **Outcome survey (UK AFCAS model).** Track pension satisfaction and understanding annually as an intermediate outcome.

## Gaps and items not verified
