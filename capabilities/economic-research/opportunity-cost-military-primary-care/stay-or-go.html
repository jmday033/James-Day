Civilian job
Starting value: 5%, a planning assumption. Someone who leaves now has years of civilian seniority, partnership, or productivity by the time a 20-year retiree starts over, and clinical skills can fade in late-career administrative jobs. 0% assumes no difference.
First-year pay: 100% is typical for employed jobs with a guaranteed starting salary; productivity-based jobs often pay less in year one. Real pay growth: 0%. Assumptions and sources

Constant 2026 dollars; year-end cash flows. Navy pay: 2026 basic pay, BAH for the duty ZIP, BAS, FY 2026 incentive pay and four-year retention bonus for the specialty (renewed through 20, one agreement, or none), $8,000 board certification pay, BRS continuation pay if selected. Specialties without their own Navy row use the subspecialty category noted beside them; surgical subspecialties are assumed to be Category I. Civilian pay: Doximity 2026 national averages by specialty. Promotions: O-5 at 15 and O-6 at 21 years after commissioning. BAH and BAS are tax-free.

Taxes: 2026 federal brackets, standard deduction, and Child Tax Credit; Social Security and Medicare; 2026 state wage-tax sample; California disability insurance on civilian pay in California. The pension is taxed at the rate entered.

Pension: 2% (BRS) or 2.5% (Legacy) × 20 years × the average of the last three years of basic pay, paid for the life-table years, discounted at the pension rate. Retiree TRICARE, VA offset or concurrent receipt, and the Survivor Benefit Plan follow the settings above. The answer assumes the stay path reaches 20. After 20, the retiree's civilian pay is reduced by the percentage entered (default 5%) until the work-until age, compared with someone who left earlier. Years still under obligation are not counted, because both paths are in the Navy then.
