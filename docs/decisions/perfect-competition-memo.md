# Perfect Competition — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant 10 tomato beds, 20 carrot beds, and 30 mesclun beds. This mix produces estimated seasonal profit of $42,761.66 while leaving four beds unplanted and remaining within the temporary-labor limit.

## Why

The eleventh tomato bed would cost $9,390.72 but earn only $8,800, reducing profit by $590.72. The twentieth carrot bed and thirtieth mesclun bed remain profitable at the margin, so both crops should be planted to their caps. Carrots and mesclun also cover their variable costs and contribute toward the farm's single $20,000 fixed cost, even though neither crop would cover that full cost alone.

## The judgment call

If crop-specific ground can be added without changing other assumptions, expand carrot capacity first. The next permitted carrot bed is worth about $352 in incremental profit, compared with about $246 for the next mesclun bed. I would pay no more than those amounts per seasonal bed of added capacity. More general land and another temporary worker have no current value because four beds and 0.835 worker-equivalents are already unused.

## What would change my answer

I would revisit the mix if prices, wage rates, crop-specific caps, or the labor function changed. Tomato price is the most immediate trigger: a fall below the tenth bed's $8,248.59 marginal cost would reduce the tomato allocation, while additional carrot capacity costing less than roughly $352 per bed would justify expansion.
