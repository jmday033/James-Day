# Stay or go? adds goals translation and exit windows

At the author's request, two counseling questions were added after the financial answer: what the difference would buy, and whether the decision can be reversed later.

**Goals panel ("Does the difference change your plans?").** The after-tax present-value difference is expressed as years of household spending, children's college, debt payoff, and a second home. College uses College Board 2025–26 average full budgets ($30,990 public in-state; $65,470 private nonprofit). Household spending defaults to $120,000, a labeled planning assumption. The panel also separates timing: extra civilian cash before 20 versus the after-tax pension from the retirement age, with the savings that would produce the same gross income at a 4% initial withdrawal rate (Bengen, 1994). The page advises using the panel before deciding, not only afterward to confirm a choice.

**Exit panel ("Can you change your mind later?").** For each year from the decision point to 20, the page shows whether the physician is free to leave and the after-tax present value of staying until then and leaving, compared with leaving now. Locks come from the current obligation, 4-year retention-bonus agreements (renewed or single), and BRS continuation pay (4 years from the 12th year, concurrent with other obligations, per MCCareer 2020; to be confirmed with BUMED Special Pays). The decision point itself is always open. An optional later civilian salary and start year (for example, after a Navy fellowship) applies only to exits at or after that year.

**Not modeled:** fellowship pay or GME payback, 2- and 3-year bonus rates, investment returns on the difference, and any pay cut for leaving before 20. The headline, the four paths, and the break-even chart are unchanged.
