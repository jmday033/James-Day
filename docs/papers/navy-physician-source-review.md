# Source review: Navy primary-care physician opportunity cost

Reviewed September 19, 2026. Companion to the [paper](navy-primary-care-physician-retention-draft.md).

## Scope and use

This record screens all 73 distinct links in the supplied list (including the Special Pays archive and three pay-guidance PDFs). It records relevant content and the decision to use, qualify, or exclude it. Repeated citations to the same URL count once. Review of an announcement or link-only post does not imply validation of every external article or attachment it references. Podcasts were reviewed through their published text, not audio.

Official pay guidance controls rate and eligibility claims. Blog posts provide historical context and personal experience; they do not identify causal retention effects. Historical salary illustrations and pension values are not converted into current inputs without matching the officer, specialty, period, benefits, and decision date.

## What changed and what remains unresolved

The paper now explains source quality, adds a documented historical FY25 special-pay comparison, differentiates actual Navy rates from ceilings, and expands pension timing, taxes, extra work, accession-versus-retention decisions, and balanced benefits communication. The advanced-practitioner staffing option remains a hypothesis requiring its own evidence.

The supplied sources do not validate the original $239,000 Navy total or the three civilian figures. The next empirical step is to specify the officer profile and actual contract, verify applicable Navy pay components, and obtain matched civilian compensation observations. The original numerical tables remain clearly labeled illustrations. Neither a current compensation gap nor the effectiveness of larger bonuses, advertising, or staffing substitution has been empirically established by this review.

### Integration boundaries
